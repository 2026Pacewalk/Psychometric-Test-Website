**Quick answer:** RIASEC is a career-interest model created by psychologist John Holland that sorts people and careers into six types — Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, and Conventional. Your top types form a "Holland Code" that points directly to careers you're likely to enjoy and succeed in.

Most career advice focuses on what you *can* do. RIASEC focuses on something just as important — what you'll actually *enjoy* doing for years. And enjoyment, it turns out, is one of the strongest predictors of career success.

If you've ever wondered why one student loves lab work while another can't wait to organise an event, RIASEC explains it. It's the backbone of interest-based career matching in our [psychometric reports](/blog/what-is-a-psychometric-test) at [TestPsychometric.com](/), operated by **AMG Educational Charitable Society**.

This guide breaks down all six types in plain language and shows how your code guides real decisions.

## What is RIASEC (and who created it)?

RIASEC was developed by American psychologist **John L. Holland** in the 1950s and refined over decades. His insight was simple but powerful: people and work environments can both be described using the same six categories. When a person's type matches their work environment, they're more satisfied, more productive, and more likely to stay.

Today RIASEC (also called the **Holland Codes**) is used by schools, universities, and career services worldwide. It's one of the most validated frameworks in career psychology — which is exactly why we use it.

## The 6 RIASEC types explained

Each person is a blend of all six, but usually two or three stand out. Here's what each type means.

### R — Realistic (the "Doers")

Practical, hands-on, and action-oriented. They like working with tools, machines, plants, animals, or the outdoors, and prefer doing over discussing.
**Careers:** engineering, agriculture, defence, sports, automobile, electrician, pilot.

### I — Investigative (the "Thinkers")

Analytical, curious, and scientific. They love solving problems, researching, and understanding how things work.
**Careers:** research, medicine, data science, software, lab technology, economics.

### A — Artistic (the "Creators")

Imaginative, expressive, and original. They dislike rigid rules and love creating — through visuals, words, music, or performance.
**Careers:** design, architecture, writing, film, fashion, music, advertising.

### S — Social (the "Helpers")

Warm, supportive, and people-focused. They enjoy teaching, helping, and working with others.
**Careers:** teaching, counselling, psychology, healthcare, social work, HR.

### E — Enterprising (the "Persuaders")

Confident, ambitious, and persuasive. They like leading, selling, and starting things.
**Careers:** business, sales, marketing, law, entrepreneurship, management, politics.

### C — Conventional (the "Organisers")

Careful, methodical, and detail-oriented. They like structure, accuracy, and clear systems.
**Careers:** accounting, banking, administration, data management, auditing, logistics.

## What is a Holland Code?

Nobody is purely one type. Your **Holland Code** is your top two or three types combined — for example:

- **SAE** (Social-Artistic-Enterprising) → a born teacher, trainer, or creative communicator.
- **IRC** (Investigative-Realistic-Conventional) → a methodical engineer or analyst.
- **EAS** (Enterprising-Artistic-Social) → an entrepreneur in a creative field.

This short code is a remarkably accurate summary of what kind of work will keep you motivated. A [psychometric assessment](/individual) calculates it for you automatically.

## How RIASEC helps students choose careers and streams

RIASEC answers the question marks can't: *what kind of work will I enjoy?*

- A strong **Realistic + Investigative** code leans toward Science and engineering.
- A strong **Enterprising + Conventional** code leans toward Commerce and business.
- A strong **Artistic + Social** code leans toward Humanities, design, and people-focused careers.

When students choose [streams after Class 10](/blog/career-guidance-after-10th) using their RIASEC code, they pick subjects they'll actually engage with — which usually improves their marks too. Interest and effort reinforce each other.

## RIASEC + aptitude + intelligence = the full picture

RIASEC is powerful, but it measures only one dimension — interest. The best career decisions combine three things:

## Real example: the same subject, two different fits

Two Class 11 students in Mohali both loved biology. RIASEC told them apart. **Priya's** code was Investigative-Realistic — perfect for research and lab medicine. **Aman's** was Social-Enterprising — he loved biology but loved *people* more. His best fit was healthcare management and public health, not the lab. Same subject, two very different careers — revealed by interest type.

## Common mistakes with RIASEC

1. **Treating it as the whole answer.** RIASEC is interest only. Pair it with ability and talent.
2. **Forcing a single type.** You're a blend. The code (two or three letters) matters more than one label.
3. **Ignoring low types.** A low score isn't a flaw — it just means that kind of work energises you less.
4. **Reading the code without the careers.** The value is in matching your code to real, current career families.

## Expert tips for using your RIASEC results

- **Start with your top two letters** and explore careers that combine them.
- **Look for overlap** between your RIASEC careers and your strongest [intelligences](/blog/multiple-intelligence-test-explained) — that overlap is your sweet spot.
- **Talk to someone in the field.** Interest is best confirmed by real exposure.
- **Re-assess in a few years.** Interests sharpen with experience.
