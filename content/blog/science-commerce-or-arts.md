Few decisions divide an Indian family like the question that arrives at the end of Class 10: *Science, Commerce, or Arts?* Father wants Science. The cousin who "settled abroad" recommends Commerce. The child quietly wants something else and says nothing.

Everyone is guessing. And a guess is a terrible basis for a decision this big.

## Why the Science-Commerce-Arts debate goes wrong

Most families decide the stream using the worst possible inputs:

- **Marks.** "She got 90%, so Science." But marks measure exam performance, not interest or aptitude for a field.
- **Prestige.** "Science is for toppers." This false ranking pushes thousands of students into a stream that doesn't suit them.
- **Other people.** Relatives advise from *their* experience, which has nothing to do with *your* child's brain.
- **Fear.** "Arts has no scope." Completely outdated — Arts leads to law, civil services, design, and psychology.

The result is a stream chosen for everyone except the student it's meant to serve.

## What actually predicts the right stream

Three things determine whether a stream will work for a child — and none of them is marks alone.

1. **Aptitude** — the natural ability a stream demands (logical reasoning for Science, organisation for Commerce, verbal and social skills for Arts).
2. **Interests** — mapped through [RIASEC](/blog/riasec-career-assessment-guide), what kind of work your child will actually enjoy.
3. **Intelligences** — your child's strongest of the [eight multiple intelligences](/blog/multiple-intelligence-test-explained).

A [psychometric test](/blog/career-guidance-after-10th) measures all three and shows where they point. That's a decision built on evidence, not emotion.

## Which stream suits which child?

**Science suits** students with strong logical-mathematical ability and investigative curiosity — those who love understanding *how* and *why* things work. Leads to engineering, medicine, research, IT.

**Commerce suits** students with enterprising and organised personalities who like business, money, and systems. Leads to CA, finance, management, entrepreneurship.

**Arts/Humanities suits** students with social, artistic, or linguistic strengths who are curious about people, society, and creativity. Leads to law, civil services, psychology, journalism, design.

> **The truth no one says aloud:** There is no "best" stream. There is only the best stream *for your child* — and a psychometric test finds it.

## Common parent mistakes (and how to avoid them)

- **Deciding for the child instead of with them.** Ownership drives motivation.
- **Equating marks with fit.** They're different things.
- **Ruling out Arts by reflex.** It's a powerful path for the right student.
- **Leaving it till the last minute.** Assess in Class 9–10, not during board stress.
